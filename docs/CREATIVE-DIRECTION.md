# Creative direction

Show a real team turning a bounded brief into two inspectable video candidates. The example campaign promotes Software Factory; the walkthrough explains the VideoOps Factory that makes that campaign. Keep those two subjects distinct.

## Three storylines

1. **Follow the work.** Start with the brief, follow the role handoffs, show both rendered formats, and end at the exact-file review gate. Choose this for the walkthrough: it gives viewers evidence of the factory they can reuse.
2. **One campaign, two frames.** Lead with the landscape and portrait outputs, then trace their shared facts and assets. Useful for a shorter release clip, but it leaves less room to explain review.
3. **Keep the evidence.** Follow one source claim through planning, rendering and review. Useful for an engineering audience, but too narrow for this first introduction.

Proceed with the first storyline. No additional creative approval pause is needed for this preparation; final media review remains separate.

## Example campaign

The two JSON briefs define 12-second silent autoplay clips at 30 fps: landscape 1920 × 1080 and portrait 1080 × 1920. Each has 360 frames, split into a two-second hook, four seconds of Factory Floor evidence, three seconds of converter evidence and a three-second Kujo bumper. These are production targets, not measured execution times.

“Your idea. A tested app.” opens the supported local software workflow. “Reviewed contracts. Frozen tests.” connects that promise to the actual mechanism. Use the real viewer and converter screenshots. The source README and pack guide support reviewed JSON packs, frozen tests, independent acceptance, Node HTTP, optional SQLite and static browser apps. The qualification record identifies a completed converter mission. A seal proves which bytes were reviewed; it does not prove good test coverage or support for arbitrary software.

Use monochrome frames, simple cuts, restrained punch-ins and Departure Mono. Reframe the portrait version around readable evidence. Do not squeeze a desktop screenshot into an unreadable strip. Do not add scene names, captions, top labels, lower thirds, fake dashboards or decorative progress counters. Short campaign headlines and the final CTA are intentional content, not transcript captions.

End with the official K mark, “Kujo” in Departure Mono, “Build your next factory with Kujo” and `kujolang.ai`. Hold the bumper long enough to read it. The examples are silent by design.

## Walkthrough narration

The draft in `.runtime/demo-script.md` targets 40–50 seconds with a natural Siren voice or an approved equivalent. Keep the delivery calm and conversational, with room for the product to register. Do not borrow the user's biography or imitate a Detroit accent. Use no caption track or editorial scene labels.

Record the real brief, saved handoffs, rendering results and review status. A replay may preserve real evidence, but it must not suggest that an edited sequence happened in real time. Show measured setup and task durations only after receipts exist, with their boundaries stated. Do not turn a render duration into a claim about total setup time.

The script describes the intended completed run. Before recording, confirm each described handoff and both outputs exist. If a gate is incomplete, preserve that state in the walkthrough. Technical checks do not establish perceptual review. Any approval must name the current candidate hash; final narration needs actual listening review as well as visual review.
